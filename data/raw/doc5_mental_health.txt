MEDICAL FAQ - MENTAL HEALTH

Q: What is mental health?
A: Mental health includes our emotional, psychological, and social well-being. It affects how we think, feel, and act, and helps determine how we handle stress, relate to others, and make choices. Mental health is important at every stage of life, from childhood and adolescence through adulthood. Mental health conditions are common — about 1 in 5 adults experiences a mental illness in any given year.

Q: What is depression?
A: Depression (major depressive disorder) is a common and serious medical illness that negatively affects how you feel, think, and act. It causes persistent feelings of sadness, loss of interest in activities once enjoyed, and can lead to a variety of emotional and physical problems. Symptoms must persist for at least two weeks for a diagnosis. Depression is one of the most treatable mental health conditions, with 80-90% of people responding positively to treatment.

Q: What are the symptoms of anxiety disorders?
A: Anxiety disorders involve more than temporary worry or fear. Symptoms include excessive worry that is difficult to control, restlessness or feeling on edge, fatigue, difficulty concentrating, irritability, muscle tension, sleep problems, rapid heartbeat, sweating, trembling, and shortness of breath. There are several types including Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD), Panic Disorder, Social Anxiety Disorder, and specific phobias. Anxiety disorders are the most common mental health conditions worldwide.

Q: How is depression treated?
A: Depression is treated with psychotherapy (especially Cognitive Behavioral Therapy or CBT), antidepressant medications (SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs, MAOIs), or a combination of both. Lifestyle changes including regular exercise, adequate sleep, healthy nutrition, and social support also play an important role. For severe or treatment-resistant depression, options include electroconvulsive therapy (ECT), transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS), and ketamine infusions.

Q: What is the difference between stress and anxiety?
A: Stress is a normal response to external pressures or threats and typically goes away when the stressor is resolved. Anxiety is a persistent sense of worry or fear that may not be tied to a specific cause and continues even after the stressor is gone. While stress is usually short-term, anxiety can be chronic and interfere significantly with daily life. Both can have physical and emotional symptoms, but anxiety disorders require clinical treatment whereas everyday stress usually resolves on its own.

Q: What is schizophrenia?
A: Schizophrenia is a serious mental disorder characterized by distorted thinking, perceptions, emotions, language, sense of self, and behavior. Symptoms include positive symptoms (hallucinations, delusions, disorganized thinking), negative symptoms (reduced motivation, flat affect, social withdrawal), and cognitive symptoms (problems with attention, memory, and executive function). It typically emerges in late adolescence or early adulthood. It is a chronic condition requiring long-term management with antipsychotic medications and psychosocial support.

Q: How can I support someone with a mental illness?
A: Supporting someone with mental illness involves educating yourself about their condition, listening without judgment, offering practical help, encouraging them to seek professional help, being patient and consistent, avoiding stigmatizing language, and taking care of your own mental health. Do not try to "fix" them or minimize their experience. Crisis resources like suicide hotlines should be known and shared when needed. Professional guidance from therapists or support groups can also help caregivers.

Q: What is PTSD?
A: Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) is a mental health condition triggered by experiencing or witnessing a terrifying event. Symptoms include intrusive memories, flashbacks, nightmares, severe anxiety, avoidance of reminders, negative changes in thinking and mood, and hyperarousal (being easily startled, sleep problems, irritability). PTSD can affect anyone, not just combat veterans. Treatment includes trauma-focused psychotherapy (Prolonged Exposure, EMDR) and medications such as SSRIs.

Q: What is bipolar disorder?
A: Bipolar disorder is a mental health condition characterized by extreme mood swings that include emotional highs (mania or hypomania) and lows (depression). During manic episodes, a person may feel unusually energetic, euphoric, or irritable. During depressive episodes, they may feel hopeless or lose interest in most activities. Bipolar disorder is a lifelong condition that is managed with mood stabilizers (lithium), anticonvulsants, antipsychotics, psychotherapy, and lifestyle management.

Q: What mental health resources are available?
A: Mental health resources include primary care physicians, psychiatrists, psychologists, licensed counselors and therapists, community mental health centers, crisis hotlines (988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline in the US), online therapy platforms, support groups (in-person and online), and employee assistance programs. Schools and universities also typically provide mental health services. Many insurance plans now cover mental health treatment, and sliding-scale fees are available at many clinics for those without insurance.
